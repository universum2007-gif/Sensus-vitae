# Sensus Vitae — Content and Scientific Editorial Policy

## 1. Educational standard

Sensus Vitae explains complex scientific ideas without making them scientifically simplistic.

Good educational content should help the reader answer:

- What is it?
- How does it work?
- Why does it happen?
- Why does it matter?
- How does it connect to other concepts?

Where useful, include:

- mechanisms;
- causal relationships;
- examples;
- analogies;
- comparisons;
- visual explanations;
- connections to related concepts;
- clear terminology;
- glossary links;
- questions for reflection or learning checks.

Detailed explanations are encouraged when detail improves understanding.

Do not shorten scientifically useful material merely to make a page look simpler.

---

## 2. Scientific integrity

Never fabricate:

- studies;
- authors;
- journals;
- statistics;
- effect sizes;
- DOI numbers;
- quotations;
- references;
- scientific consensus.

Clearly distinguish between:

- established scientific knowledge;
- individual study findings;
- hypotheses;
- theories;
- philosophical positions;
- interpretations;
- educational simplifications.

When evidence is uncertain, mixed, disputed, preliminary or changing, say so clearly.

Prefer primary scientific literature for specific factual claims when practical.

High-quality peer-reviewed reviews may be used to explain broader scientific context.

---

## 3. Scientific simplification

Educational simplification must not become scientific distortion.

A useful pattern is:

1. simple model;
2. deeper explanation;
3. important nuance.

Avoid turning complex findings into false universal rules.

When an analogy is used, make clear where necessary that it is an explanatory model rather than the literal biological or psychological mechanism.

---

## 4. Current science

Historical books and classic psychological theories may contain claims that have later been:

- replicated;
- refined;
- challenged;
- reinterpreted;
- limited to specific conditions;
- or rejected.

Where relevant, distinguish:

1. what the original author argued;
2. what evidence existed at the time;
3. what current research suggests.

For important or disputed topics, consider a section such as:

"What does current science say?"

Do not silently present an influential historical claim as current scientific consensus.

---

## 5. Books & Authors architecture

The educational library should support a scalable structure:

Club
→ Books & Authors
→ Author
→ Book
→ Chapter summaries

Robert Sapolsky is the first major collection.

Future collections may include authors such as:

- Daniel Kahneman;
- Viktor Frankl;
- and other scientifically or historically relevant authors.

Do not hard-code the architecture around one author or one book.

---

## 6. Avoid duplicate content

A single educational resource should normally have one canonical content page.

The same resource may be discoverable through multiple paths, for example:

- Books & Authors;
- Library;
- subject areas;
- glossary;
- related articles;
- search;
- internal recommendations.

Do not create multiple copies of the same article merely to place it in different sections.

Use navigation, metadata, taxonomy and internal links instead.

---

## 7. Chapter summaries

Preferred terminology:

- Chapter Summary
- Резюме главы
- equivalent natural terminology in NL and ES

A chapter summary may be detailed.

Its purpose is to help a reader understand difficult scientific or psychological material.

Where appropriate, include:

- the central idea;
- how the mechanism works;
- why it matters;
- examples;
- important terminology;
- analogies;
- causal mechanisms;
- links to related concepts;
- glossary terms;
- relevant modern scientific context;
- discussion questions;
- learning checks.

Do not remove useful explanatory detail merely because a summary is extensive.

---

## 8. Copyright and book-based educational content

The purpose of Sensus Vitae book material is to make difficult ideas easier to understand, not to reproduce the original book.

For book-based educational content:

- clearly identify the author and book;
- use independent wording;
- use independent educational structure where practical;
- add explanation, interpretation, context and learning value;
- avoid long verbatim passages;
- avoid excessive quotation;
- avoid paragraph-by-paragraph reconstruction of the original;
- avoid reproducing substantial protected text;
- do not create a near-republication of the book.

Detailed educational summaries are allowed when they are independently written and genuinely explanatory.

Do not automatically recommend shortening a summary simply because it is detailed.

If copyright risk is genuinely uncertain, flag it for owner review.

---

## 9. NotebookLM and AI-assisted preparation

NotebookLM or other AI tools may be used as preparation tools.

They are not automatically scientific sources.

Treat AI-generated educational material as a draft until reviewed.

Before publication, check:

- scientific accuracy;
- unsupported claims;
- fabricated or incorrect references;
- excessive verbatim reproduction;
- overly close reconstruction of a source;
- correct attribution;
- outdated scientific claims;
- independent educational value.

Do not present NotebookLM itself as the scientific source when the actual source is a book, paper or other publication.

---

## 10. Author-specific scientific treatment

### Robert Sapolsky

Preserve the educational value and depth of the chapter summaries.

Where modern research has refined or challenged claims discussed in the book, distinguish Sapolsky's presentation from later scientific evidence.

Do not rewrite chapters merely to make them shorter.

### Daniel Kahneman

Classic findings in cognitive and social psychology may have undergone replication or methodological reassessment.

Where relevant, explain:

- the original claim;
- later replication evidence;
- criticism or methodological limitations;
- current interpretation.

### Viktor Frankl

Clearly distinguish:

- historical and biographical narrative;
- philosophical ideas;
- logotherapy as a therapeutic framework;
- clinical claims;
- empirical scientific evidence.

Do not present a philosophical position as experimentally established fact.

---

## 11. Glossary

Sensus Vitae should use a reusable multilingual glossary for difficult scientific and psychological terminology.

Glossary entries should:

- be concise but meaningful;
- explain the concept in understandable language;
- preserve scientific accuracy;
- use the current page language;
- connect related concepts when useful.

Avoid duplicating separate definitions unnecessarily across articles.

Where practical, link difficult terms in articles to reusable glossary entries without making reading disruptive.

---

## 12. Psychology and health content

Psychology, neuroscience and health-related material on Sensus Vitae is educational.

Do not present educational content as personalized:

- diagnosis;
- medical treatment;
- psychotherapy;
- medication advice;
- individual clinical assessment.

Do not diagnose identifiable people from descriptions, photographs, messages or behavioral examples.

When discussing disorders or treatments, distinguish educational information from individualized professional care.

---

## 13. Editorial goal

Sensus Vitae should not merely tell readers facts.

It should help them build a connected mental model.

Whenever possible, help the reader understand:

- what the concept means;
- how it connects to previous knowledge;
- where the evidence comes from;
- what remains uncertain;
- and why the concept matters.
